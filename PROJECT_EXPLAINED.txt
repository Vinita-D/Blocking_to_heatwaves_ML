THE PROJECT IN PLAIN WORDS

THE QUESTION
Forecasters think the key thing about a block is how long it stays. So: does a long block bring
a heatwave? And if a heatwave comes, does the block tell us how hot, long or large it will be?

THE DATA
Two lists from the paper: blocks, and heatwaves with the blocks linked to them (a block and a
heatwave are linked if they overlap, with the block up to two days earlier). The project turns
this into one row per block: its persistence, size, strength, place, time of year, recurrence,
and the heatwave it brought (if any).

THE TOOLS
Linear regression: one formula, answer = a x persistence + b x size + ... + constant.
Logistic regression: the same, for yes/no questions. The answer is a chance, 0 to 100%.
Random forest: a game of 20 questions ("lasts over 10 days? north of 60N?"). Each chain of
  answers leads to a group of similar past blocks, and the prediction is what they did. 500 such
  trees are averaged.
Fair test: the model never sees the summer it is asked about, like an exam with new questions.

THE SCORES
Pair test: pick one block with a heatwave and one without. How often does the model give the
  heatwave block the higher chance? 50% = pure guessing (like flipping a coin), 100% = perfect.
R2: how much of the differences between heatwaves the model explains. 0 = no better than
  guessing the average. 1 = perfect. Negative = worse than guessing.

THE THREE STEPS
1. Build the table, one row per block.
2. The study. Part 1 counts: how often does each persistence class bring a heatwave? Part 2 asks
   the computer: does knowing more than the persistence help?
3. Pick a date. Which blocks existed, how likely was each to bring a heatwave, what happened?

WHAT THE RESULTS SAY
- 15% of summer blocks come with a heatwave. More persistent blocks do more often: 8% up to
  6 days, 31% over 15 days.
- Persistence alone picks the right block in 59% of pairs (guessing: 50%). All block properties
  together: 64% to 68%. So persistence helps, and size, strength and position add a little.
- Once a heatwave comes, persistence says nothing about its intensity, duration or area. All
  block properties together explain about 17% of the intensity and 24% of the area, and none of
  the duration.
- 27 June 2021: of three blocks, the model gave the highest chance (38%) to the one over North
  America, which brought the Pacific Northwest heatwave.

THE MESSAGE
How long a block lasts is linked to WHETHER a heatwave comes, not to WHAT IT IS LIKE. This
agrees with the paper. Intensity, duration and area need other ingredients: soil moisture,
surface fluxes, subtropical ridges, local processes.

LIMITS
- Longer blocks have more time to meet a heatwave by chance, so part of the link is expected.
- Only about 120 blocks came with a heatwave, so the heatwave results are rough.
- The numbers depend on the detection settings. Block persistence is assumed known.
